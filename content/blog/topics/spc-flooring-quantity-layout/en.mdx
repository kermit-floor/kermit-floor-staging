To order SPC flooring, measure the area that will actually receive the floor, allow for cuts in the agreed layout, and round the result up to complete cartons. Keep a separate decision for spare stock. These are different quantities: the room area, the installation allowance and the amount you buy should each remain visible on the order.

The calculation below is a planning worksheet. **Its 8% allowance and 2.20 m² carton coverage are hypothetical inputs, not Kermit packing specifications or a universal waste recommendation.** Replace them with your installer's layout estimate and the selected product's confirmed carton coverage.

## Measure the floor in sections

Draw each room, label the dimensions and divide irregular shapes into rectangles. Measure in metres before multiplying length by width. Include recesses that will receive flooring and avoid counting the same doorway or connecting space twice. Check the drawing against the actual site; an estate-agent floor area can include spaces outside your flooring scope.

| Example space | Calculation | Net area |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Living room | 5.00 × 4.00 m | 20.00 m² |
| Bedroom | 3.50 × 3.00 m | 10.50 m² |
| Hallway | 4.00 × 1.20 m | 4.80 m² |
| Total | Sum of the three areas | **35.30 m²** |

Record fixed cabinetry, columns, pipes and thresholds on the drawing even when their footprint seems small. Agree which areas receive flooring before subtracting them. Kermit's floating floor must remain free to move: a kitchen island or fixed cabinet should not be used to clamp it in place. The [installation planning guide](/blog/kermit-spc-flooring-installation-planning) explains the preparation and movement checks.

## Turn square metres into cartons

Use this sequence for each distinct product or batch requirement:

1. **Allowance area = net area × allowance percentage ÷ 100.**
2. **Installation order area = net area + allowance area.**
3. **Cartons = installation order area ÷ confirmed m² per carton, rounded up to a whole carton.**
4. **Purchased area = cartons × m² per carton.**
5. Add separately agreed reserve cartons if unopened spare stock is required.

For the example, 35.30 × 8% gives 2.824 m² for cuts. The installation order area is 38.124 m². Dividing by the illustrative 2.20 m² per carton gives 17.329… cartons, so the order becomes **18 cartons, covering 39.60 m²**.

The 4.30 m² difference between purchased and net area is not all spare flooring: the layout is expected to consume some in cuts. Carton rounding adds 1.476 m² above the allowance calculation, but that does not guarantee a usable set of full planks after installation. If the brief requires one unopened reserve carton, show **18 installation cartons + 1 reserve carton = 19 cartons / 41.80 m²**. Label the reserve for handover rather than letting it disappear into the cutting allowance.

## Let the layout set the allowance

A straight run in a rectangular room, a diagonal layout and a pattern with special left/right elements do not produce the same offcuts. Doorways, narrow corridors, angled walls and separate rooms can also reduce reuse. A leftover piece is useful only when its length, end profile and position suit the selected locking system and installation instructions.

Ask the installer to mark the starting line, plank direction, transitions and approximate first and last rows. Compare two orientations before choosing purely on appearance: a direction that looks attractive from the entrance may create many short cuts around recesses. Use the [colour and format guide](/blog/choose-spc-flooring-colour-format) to compare actual collection formats; a room image alone does not establish that a particular pattern is available.

A width calculation illustrates why the last row matters. The published 6 mm no-IXPE technical sheet gives a plank width of **228.6 mm**. In a hypothetical room exactly 4,000 mm wide, two minimum 10 mm perimeter gaps leave 3,980 mm. Seventeen full widths use 3,886.2 mm, leaving a 93.8 mm strip. An alternative layout with sixteen full rows and two 161.2 mm edge rows also totals 3,980 mm. This is geometry, not an approved laying pattern: the installer still checks wall irregularities, minimum cut sizes and the product's joint instructions.

## Copy this order worksheet

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Product | Collection, decor code, dimensions, thickness, wear layer and backing |
| Measured area | Each room and the agreed exclusions |
| Layout | Direction, pattern, thresholds and fixed objects |
| Cut allowance | Percentage and why it suits this layout |
| Pack coverage | Confirmed m² per carton for this exact configuration |
| Installation cartons | Rounded-up quantity |
| Reserve | Separate full cartons or agreed full planks |
| Accessories | Skirting in linear metres; transitions by opening and profile |
| Handover | Batch references, spare-stock location and care instructions |

Do not convert plank dimensions into a carton quantity without the confirmed plank count. Do not use a flooring m² total as the skirting order: measure the relevant wall runs, exclude openings and plan profile lengths and corners separately. Flooring, skirting and wall panels need their own quantity schedules.

For a complete budget, carry the rounded purchased area into the [SPC flooring cost worksheet](/blog/spc-flooring-cost), while keeping labour and preparation on the area basis actually quoted. This prevents paying twice for an allowance that was already included in a package price.

```faq
## Quantity and layout questions

### How much extra SPC flooring should I order?

Use an allowance based on the actual room shapes, pattern and reusable cuts. The 8% in this article is an example, not a fixed Kermit recommendation. Confirm the layout with the installer before rounding to cartons.

### How many square metres are in a Kermit carton?

Request the packing specification for the selected collection and configuration. The 2.20 m² carton used here is hypothetical and should not be used to place an order.

### Can I count cut waste as future spare stock?

Not reliably. Offcuts may be too short or have the wrong usable edge. If you need unopened cartons for later repairs, list them separately and keep the product and batch details.

### Does a movement gap reduce the amount I should buy?

Movement gaps belong in the installation layout. Do not reduce an already tight order by estimating their small area; calculate cuts and whole-carton rounding together. Kermit's manual requires at least 10 mm around walls and fixed objects, with additional joints assessed for the layout.
```
