If you are asking **what makes Kermit SPC skirting different**, the most practical answer is:

**The difference is not only the profile shape. It is the finish quality at the wall-floor line, the flexible-edge gasket detail, and a skirting system approach that is designed for real project conditions.**

In real jobs, the “difference” is usually seen in:

- how clean the wall-floor line looks after installation
- how much corrective silicone work is needed
- how the surface behaves in cleaning and daily impact
- how consistent the color/finish remains over time
- how quickly installers can complete details and hand over the project

This article translates these technical and commercial differentiators into a **practical selection guide** for dealers, installers, architects, and project buyers.

## 1. The biggest visible difference: flexible top and bottom gasket edges

One of the strongest differentiators in Kermit SPC skirting positioning is the **flexible edge / gasket detail** (top and bottom edge contact).

Why this matters:

- walls are rarely perfectly straight
- floors are rarely perfectly level along the whole run
- small irregularities create visible gaps after skirting installation
- many installers correct those gaps with a separate silicone bead

Kermit already highlights **Flexible Edges** in its skirting feature set, and Turkish marketing copy also frames this as a solution that can reduce the need for mastik/silicone finishing in the joint.

In practical terms, this means the profile is designed to:

- sit more cleanly against imperfect walls
- improve the visual contact line at the floor edge
- reduce the appearance of shadow gaps
- lower silicone dependency in many standard installations

## 2. Difference vs many generic skirtings: less dependency on post-install silicone correction

This is a real field observation:

> In many skirting systems, visible wall gaps are corrected with silicone after mounting.

That is why a **flexible edge skirting board** is not just a cosmetic talking point. It changes the finishing workflow.

**Kermit SPC skirting is positioned to reduce or eliminate the need for a visible silicone line in many standard installations by helping cover gaps caused by wall/floor unevenness.**

Kermit is the skirting solution that best compensates for errors in the key quality-defining factors below during skirting installation:

- wall condition
- floor flatness
- cut quality
- alignment
- corner detailing

That credibility matters in B2B decisions.

## 3. Surface and color layer: why users care about “scratch appearance” and finish continuity

Another major differentiator is the **surface/color layer behavior**.

In project conversations, buyers often ask not only “Does it scratch?” but:

- **If it gets marked, does the color appearance change visibly?**
- **Does it need touch-up/repair work?**
- **Will the finish look tired quickly in high-use areas?**

Kermit SPC skirting does not use a paint or foil surface color layer; it has an integral/original color layer. This is why scratching does not create the same visible color-change contrast seen in painted or foil-covered surfaces, and why the profile offers high scratch resistance with stronger color continuity.

This matters especially in high-use interiors, because the skirting keeps a cleaner and more consistent appearance for longer while reducing repair/touch-up needs.
